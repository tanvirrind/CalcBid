# CalcBid business strategy

*Authored by Tanveer, pasted into chat 2026-10-06. Updated with his later decisions (multi-trade pivot 2026-10-06).*

## 1. Strategic position

CalcBid should not launch as another all-in-one contractor CRM. Joist and Jobber already cover estimates, invoices, payments, scheduling, and customer management at scale. [Joist](https://www.joist.com/) positions itself around the full estimate-to-payment workflow, while [Jobber](https://www.getjobber.com/industries/general-contracting/) emphasizes estimates, approvals, scheduling, portals, and payments.

CalcBid’s wedge should be:

> Trade-accurate estimating for small contractors who still use spreadsheets, notes, or generic quote templates.

Rank the free calculators for SEO in every trade covered, and convert calculator users into paying customers from any trade. No per-trade customer gate — expand trade coverage based on SEO traction and customer demand.

## 2. Ideal customer

*Initial market: United States — units, pricing, and trade conventions throughout this doc are US-based.*

Primary customer:

- Solo contractors and small crews (painters, roofers, tile & flooring installers)
- 1–10 employees
- Residential and light commercial work
- 10–100 estimates per month
- Currently using Excel, Google Sheets, paper, or generic invoicing tools
- Wants faster bids and fewer pricing mistakes
- Does not want a complicated CRM

Secondary customers:

- Handymen
- Drywall contractors
- Deck and fence crews
- HVAC contractors

Avoid initially:

- Large commercial contractors
- Multi-location companies
- Businesses needing dispatch, payroll, accounting, or complex project management

## 3. Core promise

The product promise should become:

> Measure the job, price it profitably, and send a quote in under 10 minutes.

The current “calculate the job, send the quote, get paid” message is strong, but “get paid” should become a later-stage feature rather than the core launch claim. Estimates and invoices are different workflow objects, as QuickBooks explicitly distinguishes. [QuickBooks](https://quickbooks.intuit.com/payments/invoicing/generator/)

## 4. Product strategy

### Free acquisition product

Keep the free calculators (paint, roofing, and tile & flooring are live):

- Trade-specific inputs (room dimensions, pitch, tile size, waste factors)
- Materials and labor
- Job total
- Shareable result
- SEO landing pages for specific searches

Examples:

- Paint calculator for contractors
- How many gallons of paint for a room
- Roofing squares calculator
- How many tiles do I need
- Roof replacement cost calculator
- Floor tile cost estimator

### Paid product

The paid product must go beyond a generic quote builder:

- Saved customers and jobs
- Saved material and labor rates
- Overhead and markup settings
- Quote templates
- Branded PDFs
- Quote status: draft, sent, viewed, approved, declined
- Automated follow-ups
- Change orders
- Convert approved quote into invoice
- Payment links
- Basic job profitability reporting

The highest-value feature is not “a nicer quote.” It is reliable trade-specific pricing that protects margin.

### Recommended roadmap

| Phase | Product focus |
|---|---|
| 0–3 months | Accounts, saved quotes, customer records, markup/overhead, branded PDF |
| 3–6 months | Quote tracking, approval links, follow-up reminders, invoice conversion |
| 6–12 months | Payments, change orders, reusable estimating templates, team accounts |
| 12+ months | Decks, HVAC, drywall, concrete, supplier integrations |

Do not build scheduling, payroll, full accounting, or a general CRM until customers repeatedly request them.

## 5. Pricing

The current $19 / $49 concept is reasonable, but the plans should be tied to outcomes.

### Free — $0

- Calculators
- 3 quotes per month
- Basic quote export
- No saved customer history

### Solo — $19/month

- Unlimited quotes
- Saved customers and jobs
- Branding
- Templates
- Quote tracking
- Follow-up reminders
- Margin and markup controls

### Crew — $49/month

- Multiple users
- Shared templates and rate cards
- Team permissions
- Job profitability
- Change orders
- Priority support

This sits below more expansive contractor platforms while offering more specialized estimating value. Joist’s own comparison content places entry-level contractor software around the low monthly tens, while larger systems commonly begin higher. [Joist pricing comparison](https://www.joist.com/workshop/compare/best-estimating-apps-for-small-contractors-2026/)

Use annual pricing only after retention is proven:

- Solo annual: $190/year
- Crew annual: $490/year

## 6. Go-to-market

### Channel 1: Search-driven calculators

This should be the primary acquisition channel.

Build highly specific calculator pages with:

- Instant calculator
- Example calculation
- Explanation of assumptions
- Downloadable estimate template
- CTA: “Turn this into a professional quote”

Every calculator should capture an email only after delivering value.

### Channel 2: Contractor education

Publish short practical content:

- How to price a painting job
- Markup versus margin
- How much overhead to add
- How to estimate labor hours
- How to stop underbidding
- What to include in a painting quote
- How to follow up on an unanswered estimate

This aligns with the industry’s recurring pain around materials, labor, overhead, and markup. [Joist pricing guide](https://www.joist.com/workshop/estimating-pricing/contractor-pricing-guide/)

### Channel 3: Direct founder-led sales

Recruit the first 50 customers manually, from any of the live trades:

- Local painting, roofing, and tile/flooring companies
- Facebook contractor groups
- Trade associations
- Paint and tile stores
- Contractor coaches
- YouTube creators
- Estimating consultants

Offer a 30-minute setup call and import their existing rate sheet.

### Channel 4: Partnerships

Potential partners:

- Independent paint and tile stores
- Trade consultants
- Contractor accountants
- Insurance agents
- Trade schools
- Local builder associations
- Supplier referral programs

A paint or tile store can recommend CalcBid when contractors ask how much material to buy.

## 7. Launch plan

### Phase 1 — Learn

- Interview 15 contractors across the live trades (painters, roofers, tile installers)
- Watch at least five people create real estimates
- Identify their actual estimating spreadsheet fields
- Recruit five pilot businesses

### Phase 2 — Build the paid core

- Add overhead, markup, labor burden, and room-by-room support
- Build account creation and saved quotes
- Add branded quote templates
- Add sent/viewed/approved status
- Add follow-up reminders

### Phase 3 — Charge and scale

- Charge the first customers
- Publish 10 calculator or educational pages
- Collect before/after time-saved evidence
- Launch publicly to contractors
- Publish customer case studies
- Add referral incentives
- Test $19 versus $29 pricing
- Add invoice conversion
- Begin research for the next trades (deck, HVAC, concrete)

## 8. Metrics that matter

Track the entire funnel:

1. Calculator visit
2. Calculator completion
3. Quote creation
4. Quote shared
5. Quote viewed
6. Quote approved
7. Paid conversion
8. Monthly retention
9. Estimates per active user
10. Time saved per estimate

Initial operating targets should be treated as hypotheses:

- 30%+ calculator completion
- 20%+ of completed calculators create a quote
- 50%+ of created quotes are shared
- 10%+ free-to-paid conversion among active quote users
- 80%+ month-one retention for paid pilots

The most important metric is paid users who create multiple quotes every month.

## 9. Competitive advantage

CalcBid’s defensibility should come from:

- Trade-specific calculation logic
- Customer-specific rate libraries
- Historical estimate accuracy
- Contractor pricing benchmarks
- Deep templates for each trade
- Workflow knowledge captured from real contractors

Generic quoting software can copy a quote layout. It is harder to copy years of trade-specific estimating rules and real-world pricing data.

## 10. Main risks

### Risk: becoming a generic CRM

Mitigation: keep the product centered on estimating accuracy and margin protection.

### Risk: calculator users never pay

Mitigation: limit saved quotes, templates, branding, and quote tracking to paid plans.

### Risk: incorrect estimates create liability

Mitigation:

- Clearly label assumptions
- Allow user-adjustable coverage and waste factors
- Show calculation details
- Include disclaimers
- Never present estimates as guaranteed quantities

### Risk: users already have Joist or Jobber

Mitigation: position CalcBid as the faster, more trade-accurate estimating layer, not a full replacement on day one.

### Risk: shareable URLs expose customer data

Mitigation: move shared quotes to backend-generated records with expiring or revocable links before handling sensitive production data.

## Recommended strategic decision

Build CalcBid as:

> The estimating and margin-protection system for small contractors.

Rank the free calculators for SEO in every trade covered, and win paying customers from any trade — painters, roofers, tile and flooring installers alike. Expand trade coverage based on SEO traction and customer demand, not a per-trade customer gate.
